The framework

Institutions produce outcomes through structure.

Public and private institutions are often judged by their declared purpose. CGF starts elsewhere: with the mechanisms through which authority, incentives, power and dependencies shape what an institution can actually perceive, decide and sustain.

The framework examines causal structure as a connected system. Formal rules matter, but so do information asymmetries, political constraints, accumulated path dependence, feedback effects and the distribution of consequences. Governance quality depends on how these forces interact over time.

01 Structure

Decision rights, incentives, power, information and constraints.

02 Transmission

Dependencies, feedback effects, narratives and path dependence.

03 Outcome

Capacity, adaptation, burden-sharing, legitimacy and failure.

Operating logic

Four pillars for governing under pressure.

These pillars organise the present CGF approach. They are analytical commitments, not a claim that a validated operating system has already been established.

  1. 01

    Causal mapping

    Identify the mechanisms producing outcomes through decision rights, incentives, dependencies, power, information and constraints.

  2. 02

    Anticipation

    Identify transmission pathways and material second- and third-order effects early enough to preserve room for action.

  3. 03

    Adaptive optionality

    Preserve alternatives, slack, authority and capacity before efficiency-first optimisation turns uncertainty into irreversible constraint.

  4. 04

    Legitimacy

    Treat legitimacy as an operational variable affecting compliance, burden-sharing, adaptation and the ability to sustain difficult action.

Principal research programme

Causal Resilience

Causal resilience is the active capacity to detect causal structure early, understand interacting dependencies, incentives and narratives, anticipate second- and third-order effects before they compound, and reconfigure before disruption hardens into systemic failure.

The programme begins from a structural proposition: many modern institutions remain mis-specified for a more tightly coupled, contested and dependency-sensitive operating environment.

Part I Current work

Causal Resilience: Upgrading Governance for the Polycrisis Age

Part I develops the diagnostic and conceptual foundations: why tightly coupled risks expose institutional design weaknesses, how causal pathways compound, and why the preservation of room for action matters before disruption becomes systemic.

Part II In development

Causal Resilience II: Operationalising Governance in the Polycrisis Age

Part II is concerned with implementation: how states, regulators, firms and public institutions redesign authority, decision-making, anticipation, optionality and legitimacy under pressure, scarcity, incomplete information and political constraint.

Evidence & validation

Status before claims.

CGF is presented as an evolving framework and research platform. Its public language should remain proportionate to the evidence available.

A

Framework status

The current work is conceptual and developmental. No claim is made here that CGF or Causal Resilience has been externally validated or operationally proven.

B

Metrics and instruments

Any future metrics, diagnostic devices or proposed instruments should be treated as developmental heuristics, not validated measurement systems, unless and until supporting evidence is published.

C

Testing and learning

Future application should make assumptions, methods, limitations and counter-evidence visible. Claims should be revised as evidence accumulates.

Values & governance principles

Responsibility remains visible.

Sound institutional design is not only a question of prediction. It is also a question of authority, contestability, consequences and the standards by which action is justified.

Causal clarity

Distinguish mechanisms from symptoms, correlation from explanation, and stated intent from institutional effect.

Accountable authority

Make decision rights, governing standards and responsibility for consequences explicit.

Proportionality

Match the reach and reversibility of intervention to the quality of evidence and the cost of error.

Contestability

Keep assumptions open to challenge, surface alternative explanations and create routes for correction.

Adaptive stewardship

Protect capacity, institutional memory and future options rather than optimising only for immediate efficiency.

Operational legitimacy

Consider how fairness, voice and burden-sharing affect compliance and the ability to maintain action under pressure.

Ethical AI & institutional risk

Assistance without transferred authority.

AI may widen analytical capacity. It does not inherit final decision authority.

AI may support

Source organisation, pattern detection, hypothesis generation, scenario testing and analytical comparison.

Humans remain responsible for

The governing standard, the evidence relied upon, the final decision and the consequences of error.

The use of AI can also change information asymmetries, incentives and accountability. It should therefore be treated as part of the causal system being governed, not merely as a neutral analytical tool.

Policy Lab

A developmental application space.

Status: Developmental

The Policy Lab is a proposed environment for disciplined application, comparison and learning around CGF ideas.

It should not presently be read as an established operating programme, validated methodology, active pilot portfolio or proven commercial offering. Any future work would require a defined problem, responsible institutional partners, suitable evidence and transparent evaluation standards.
